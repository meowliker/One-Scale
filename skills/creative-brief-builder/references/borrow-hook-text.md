# Borrow: Hook Text Only

The strategist likes the inspiration's hook words, the line that stops the scroll. We keep the hook's mechanism, convert it for our product, then build our own way into the product.

## Steps

### 1. Read their hook and say why it works
Do the hook analysis from `breakdown.md`. Focus on:
- Exact words, word count, line breaks, spoken vs. on-screen.
- **Mechanism:** what makes it work (e.g. "identity callout + confession: 'I'm a nurse and I…'", "swap: 'Quit X, did this instead'", "specific number creates believability").
- **Who it calls out**, and the **promise or curiosity gap** it opens.
- How *they* bridge out of it (the next 1–2 lines), as a reference only.

Keep it short: the hook block plus 2–4 lines of "why it works for them".

### 2. Data card
From `product-data.md`. For this run you mostly need: buyer, top pains and desires, customer words, best existing hooks, the winner's angle, and claims we can't make.

### 3. Converted hooks H1–H6
Write 6 hooks for our product that keep the **mechanism** (not the exact words) of their hook:
- Match their word count (±2 words). Show the count on every option.
- Spread them across different pains, desires, or angles from our data, so they test different things. The mechanism stays fixed in this borrow (that's what the strategist liked), so the variety has to come from *what* the hook is about.
- If their hook relies on something we can't honestly use (a credential, a competitor product, a banned comparison), keep the shape and swap that part for something true for us. Say what you swapped and why, in one line, above the options.
- 1–2 can be "same mechanism, our winner's angle", and 1 can push a fresh angle (label it `⚠️ untested`).
- Tag each with evidence (`📊` / `🎯` / `⚠️`).
- If their hook is on-screen text, ours is too. If it's spoken, give the spoken line. If it's both, give both and keep the two separate.

Also suggest a simple **visual to put under the hook** for each (1 line). It doesn't have to copy their visual, because the strategist only borrowed the words. Default to what our winner's opening looks like.

### 4. Bridge lines BR1–BR4 + soft product intro
The bridge connects the hook's promise to our product without a hard sell. See bridge patterns in `writing-options.md`.

Show them as one table. Not every bridge fits every hook, so say which:

| ID | Bridge line | Soft product intro | Works with | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BR1 | "[bridge line]" | "[subtle product introduction line]" | H1, H3, H5 | 📊 [evidence] |

The intro should feel like a natural next step from the bridge ("…that's when my sister sent me this"), not an ad voice ("Introducing the all-new…"), unless our winner's voice is direct.

### 5. What comes after the intro
Ask in the same message (unless intake already answered it):
```
After the product intro, the rest of the video should be:
(a) our winner's body: [winner ad name]  ← default
(b) a new short body (I'll write 2 options once you pick the hook and bridge)
(c) end on CTA right after the intro (short 10–15s version)
```

### 6. Stop and ask for picks
Hooks (one or several for V1/V2/V3), bridge + intro, and the after-intro choice. Wait.

If they pick (b), write 2 short bodies (B1–B2) and stop again for a pick before the brief.

### 7. Brief
Use `editor-brief-template.md`. For (a), the body rows come from the winner's script. If the data file doesn't have the winner's script and shots, ask the strategist for it, or describe the body as "same as [winner ad], from 0:0X onward" with the timestamp to cut from.
