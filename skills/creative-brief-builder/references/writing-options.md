# Writing Options: Hooks, Bridges, Bodies

## Numbering and layout

| Prefix | What |
|---|---|
| `H1…` | Hook (text and/or spoken) |
| `VH1…` | Visual hook version |
| `BR1…` | Bridge (+ soft product intro) |
| `B1…` | Body script |
| `F1…` | Format fill (concept) |
| `C1…` | CTA |

Show options as compact tables, not paragraphs:

```
HOOKS: match their text hook: 6 words (±2)
| ID | On-screen text | Spoken (if any) | Words | Mechanism | Visual under it | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | "Quit coffee. / Slept 8 hours." | — | 5 | Swap | Hand pushes mug away, overhead | 📊 customer word "finally slept" (14 reviews) |
| H2 | ... |
```

Put the **data card** (5–8 lines) above the options so the strategist sees what they're built on.

## Hook mechanisms

Use these to make sure options really differ. Pick the mechanisms that fit the borrowed hook and our data:

| Mechanism | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Callout | "If you [situation]…", "For every [person] who…" | "For moms who dread bedtime" |
| Confession | "I [admission]" | "I hid this from my husband" |
| Swap | "Quit/Skipped X, did this instead" | "Skipped therapy. Did this instead." |
| Result-first | Outcome before the how | "She read her first book at 4" |
| Question | A question the viewer answers in their head | "Why is your kid always tired?" |
| Pattern interrupt | "Stop doing X" / unexpected statement | "Stop buying flashcards." |
| Comparison | X costs / takes … vs. this | "$150 an hour vs. 10 minutes of this" |
| Curiosity gap | Withheld key fact | "The thing teachers won't tell parents" |
| Number / list | Specific count | "3 signs your skin barrier is damaged" |
| POV | "POV: [situation or reframe]" | "POV: bedtime takes 5 minutes now" |
| Authority | "I'm a [credential] and…" | Use only if the credential is real and in the data |
| Tried-everything | "Tried X. Tried Y. This worked." | "Tried melatonin. Tried tea. Then this." |

## Hook rules

- **Word count:** match the inspiration's count for each channel (text and spoken counted separately), within ±2. Show the count. Short hooks are short for a reason: they must be readable in about 1.5–2 seconds.
- **On-screen text readability:** about 3 words per second of screen time, max 2 lines, and ~6 words per line on 9:16.
- **Text hook ≠ spoken hook** unless the inspiration uses captions that copy the VO. When both exist, the text usually carries the punch and the VO adds context. Don't make the VO read the text word for word unless the inspiration does.
- **Customer words first:** where the data has an exact buyer phrase that fits, use it.
- **No fake specifics:** a number, duration, or result in a hook must come from the data. Otherwise use a `[NEED: proof]` placeholder.

## Bridge patterns

The bridge is the 1–2 lines between the hook and the product. It pays off or deepens the hook's promise and makes the product feel like the natural answer. A weak bridge is why "great hook, no sales" happens: the hook pulls people in, then the jump to the product feels like a bait-and-switch.

| Pattern | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Story turn | "…until [moment / person] showed me this" | "…until my sister sent me this." |
| Reason why | "Turns out it's not [X], it's [Y]" | "Turns out it wasn't the caffeine. It was my evenings." |
| Agree + flip | "I thought [belief] too. Then…" | "I thought it was just stress too. Then I tried this." |
| Mechanism tease | "There's a reason [result] happens" | "There's a reason kids learn faster with pictures." |
| Proof drop | Quick credible stat or customer count, then product | "[N] parents use this every night." (only with real N) |
| Problem zoom | Make the pain specific and felt | "Every night: 40 minutes of 'one more story'." |
| Direct | Straight to the product. Use only when our winner's voice is direct. | "This is the [product]." |

**Soft product intro:** the first time the product appears, it should feel discovered or recommended, not announced. Show it in use or in hand within 1 second of the name. Say who it's for or what it does in one short clause. Hold the full benefits for the body.

Say which hooks each bridge fits (`Works with: H1, H3`). A confession hook needs a story-turn bridge; a question hook needs a reason-why bridge.

## Bodies

- Follow the beat order the borrow calls for (their structure for full copy, the skeleton for format, the winner's structure when the body comes from our winner).
- One idea per line. VO lines under ~12 words. Spoken pace about 2.5 words per second, so check the total fits the target length.
- Every claim traces to the data. Benefits in customer words where possible.
- End with the offer and CTA from the data file's standard CTA. If there isn't one, give C1–C2 options.

## Visual instructions inside options

Each hook and body row gets a visual note. When we're copying their visual, reference their shot ("= their shot 3: overhead hand demo, our product"). When we're not, describe it plainly ("Selfie, creator in car, holding product up to camera"). Simple enough to film from.
