# Borrow: Visual Hook Only

The strategist likes what the inspiration *shows* in its opening seconds: the shot, action, prop, transition, or pattern interrupt. We recreate that visual for our product, then give it words and a bridge so the editor isn't left guessing how to get into the product.

## Steps

### 1. Read the visual hook completely
From `breakdown.md`, do the hook seconds in fine detail. Split into ~0.5s steps if a lot happens:

```
| Time | What we see (very specific) | Camera / framing | Motion / transition | Sound | Text on screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:00.5 | Hand drops a full jar onto marble counter, jar bounces | Overhead, tight, static | Hard cut in, slight shake on impact | Loud thud | — |
| 0:00.5–0:01.5 | ... |
```

Then:
- **Why it stops the scroll:** the pattern interrupt, the curiosity, the satisfying action, the unexpected object, the face or emotion. 2–3 lines.
- **What makes it recognisable:** the 2–4 details that must survive in our version (e.g. "overhead + impact + sound on frame 1").
- **What it's tied to in their video:** does the visual set up something specific about *their* product that ours doesn't have? If yes, say what needs to change.

### 2. Data card
From `product-data.md`. You mostly need: product facts (what it physically looks like and how it's used), buyer, top pains and desires, winner angle, and claims we can't make.

### 3. Visual hook versions VH1–VH3
Recreate their visual for our product. Each version:

```
VH1: "[short name]"  🎯 keeps: [the recognisable details]
     Shot: [exactly what's filmed, second by second, in simple words]
     Our product's role: [where it appears / what it does in the shot]
     How to make it: [shoot UGC / product footage we have / stock / AI-generated]
     Difficulty: easy / medium / hard  (+ why)
```

Make one a **close copy** (same shot, our product) and one or two **twists** (same mechanism, adapted to how our product is used or to our winner's setting).

### 4. Words on top: hooks H1–H5
A visual hook still needs text and/or a spoken line. Write 5 options that fit the visual (the words should make the visual mean something), using our data. Tag evidence, show word counts, and keep text short enough to read during the visual (about 3 words per second on screen). Say which VH each one fits best.

If their visual hook works with no words, include one `H0: no text, visual only` option and explain when it makes sense.

### 5. Bridges BR1–BR3 + soft product intro
Same as in `borrow-hook-text.md` step 4: a bridge line plus a natural intro, saying which hooks each one fits.

### 6. What comes after
Same choice as the hook-text borrow: (a) our winner's body (default), (b) a new short body, (c) end on CTA.

### 7. Stop and ask for picks
One visual hook (or two to test), hook text(s), bridge, after-intro choice. Wait.

### 8. Brief
Use `editor-brief-template.md`. The visual hook rows must be detailed enough to film from: framing, action, motion, sound on frame 1. Put a link or timestamp to the inspiration's hook in the brief so the editor can watch the reference ("copy the camera move at 0:00–0:02 of [link]").
